Captain Key LargoRedfish

Redfish: copper in the shallows.

Push into the Florida Bay backcountry and the water gets skinny, quiet, and alive. Redfish root through the flats with their backs half out of the water, and the take is visual.

SeasonYear round; fall shines
WhereFlorida Bay flats & edges
StyleSight fishing, light tackle
The momentWatching the eat

The backcountry behind Key Largo is a different world from the reef. Miles of flats, creeks, and basins in Everglades National Park water, where the game is stalking fish you can see: redfish tailing on a shallow flat, pushing wakes across a basin, or crawling a bank with their copper shoulders out of the water.

This is Brian's home water and his favorite kind of guiding: reading tide and light, staking off the right edge, and putting a bait or lure where a feeding fish will find it. When a redfish eats in a foot of water, you see everything. Nobody forgets their first one.

The honest backcountry

Sight fishing is hunting, and hunting has slow days. Wind, light, and tide decide a lot. What you get with fifty years of local knowledge is more shots per day than luck would ever give you, and snook, trout, and small tarpon share the same water, so the backcountry rarely sends anyone home empty.

Quick answers

When is the best redfish season?

They are in the bay year round; fall usually brings the most consistent tailing and schooling fish.

Is this fly-fishing only?

No. Spin, bait, and fly all work; Brian rigs the boat to the crew's experience.

What else lives in the backcountry?

Snook, sea trout, small tarpon, sharks, and more. It is a mixed bag by nature.
